Global Trade, Local Jobs, and the Rise of Trump 1.0

with Jun Pan and Jiatao Liu

Presentations: Fudan International School of Finance (2026); CKGSB (2026)

Abstract

This paper examines how the uneven distribution of globalization’s benefits and costs contributed to the rise of Trump 1.0. Central to our analysis are two opposing local economic experiences of globalization: efficiency gains from global sourcing and job losses from manufacturing dislocations. Using the two-party pro-Republican vote share (R-Vote), we document a substantial pro-Trump shift in 2016 among counties with lower participation in global sourcing (IM = imports/GDP), and greater manufacturing share of local employment (L-Share). Notably, L-Share measured in the 1970s-80s – well before the China Shock – predicts the 2016 pro-Trump shift, reflecting globalization’s long shadow. The effects are economically large: one-standard-deviation cross-county differences in IM and L-Share are associated with shifts of −0.74 and +1.29 percentage points in R-Vote, respectively. Aligning the IM and L-Share effects by industry, we further document a striking contrast in electoral impact between the benefits (e.g., global sourcing in electronics) and costs (e.g., global competition in steel) of globalization. Overall, the 2016 election stands apart for the pronounced electoral shift associated with the globalization divide – an effect largely absent in other presidential elections from 2008 to 2024. The ensuing political realignment persisted through 2024, marking 2016 as a distinct political turning point.

In Search of Common Factors in Commodity Markets: A Latent Factor Perspective

with Yufeng Han, Qi Xu, and Pengcheng Zhou

Revise and resubmit, Management Science

Presentations: CFAM 2024; China Derivatives Youth Forum 2022; ICFOD 2022

Abstract

We introduce a new latent factor pricing model in commodity markets based on the risk-premium principal component analysis (RP-PCA). Using ten prominent commodity characteristics, we extract three latent factors through RP-PCA. The new model consistently outperforms the principal component analysis (PCA) model and observed three-factor and six-factor models in explaining commodity anomalies both in-sample and out-of-sample. Factor selection, factor spanning tests, and cross-sectional regressions confirm the pricing power of the new factors. RP-PCA consistently outperforms other latent factor models in pricing performance. Combining it with Instrumental PCA (IPCA) further enhances pricing individual commodity returns. The new RP-PCA based factor timing strategies also outperform various static strategies and the original PCA-based strategies. Overall, our analysis highlights the merits of employing the latent factor approach in understanding risk-return profiles in commodity markets.

Commodity Network and Predictable Returns

with Qi Xu

Journal of Futures Markets, 2023, 43(10), 1423–1449

Journal article ↗

Abstract

We investigate the lead-lag relation in the cross-section of commodity returns. We estimate dynamic and directional networks for 32 commodities and then construct a new predictor termed commodity network momentum, exploring cross-commodity information spillover. Network momentum positively and significantly predicts future commodity returns, controlling for existing characteristics. Unlike existing lead-lag studies, the predictive relation is consistent with overreaction rather than underreaction. We provide evidence that the predictive relation is stronger for more attention-grabbing commodities. Extrapolation from connected commodities contributes to this relation. Overall, our paper highlights the role of information spillover in commodity return predictability.
